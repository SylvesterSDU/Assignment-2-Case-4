Why is the sky blue? The answer to that question may be a bit complicated. To understand it, you need to know about light and why the sky appears blue. You can read more at ScienceBlogs here. There are two types of rays in sunlight: Blue and Red. If you look at the sun with an eye close up, you will notice that most of its energy (energy that comes from the Sun) travels as red rays through the atmosphere, because that’s where we see the largest amount of these molecules and atoms. These are also known as the “Blue” rays. But when the Sun shines down on the Earth, it reflects some of those red rays back into space and gets mostly absorbed by water vapor, making the sky appear blue. The amount of this reflected light depends upon how much heat has been added to the surface of the Earth. On very hot days, there might be enough heat coming off the Sun so that all the water vapors have boiled away and the sky looks blue. On cold nights, however, less heat has been added to the ground, and the sky appears gray or black. When it rains or snows, the clouds get bigger and darker, and their particles scatter the blue rays out into space until they disappear
